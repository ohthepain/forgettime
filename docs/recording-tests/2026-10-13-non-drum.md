# First supplied recording: exploratory result

The user supplied a non-drum M4A recording and confirmed an intended tempo of **110 BPM**. The sound source and exact hit annotations were not supplied at analysis time. This is a diagnostic test of the existing algorithm, not a measurement of the player's accuracy and not a drum-kit validation pass.

The original file was left unchanged. macOS `afconvert` decoded it locally to mono PCM16 at its original 48 kHz sample rate. The decoded duration is 41.3867 seconds. Audio and derived sample-level reports remain in the ignored local `output/audio-validation/2026-10-13-sample/` directory; they are not included in source control or uploaded.

Waveform inspection shows multiple candidate detections inside several broad sound bursts and many low-amplitude candidates in the quiet opening. This strongly suggests retriggering and noise sensitivity. Candidate count alone is not a confirmed false-positive count: the exact physical sound source and intended attacks need annotation.

The low-level ending section from 38.5 to 41.1 seconds still produces 16 candidates. Those keep restarting the silence timer. Final large transients occur after 41.1 seconds, so the recording does not contain a verified full-bar interval after the final detected sound. It therefore **does not independently validate automatic stopping after actual playing ends**. The timer correctly follows detected candidates, but mistaken candidates can prevent the desired pause.

The reference stays fixed once acquired, but an invariant reference does not make the detected attacks or rhythm interpretation correct. The 84 numeric scores must not be treated as a valid early/late assessment of this performance.

## Reproduce locally

```sh
# Decode without changing the original; macOS audio services may require normal host permissions.
afconvert -f WAVE -d LEI16 -c 1 /path/to/recording.m4a /path/to/recording.wav
node --import tsx scripts/validate-audio.ts /path/to/recording.wav --bpm 110 > /path/to/report.json
```

The runner now accepts macOS extensible PCM WAV files and supports an unannotated exploratory mode. In that mode, precision, recall and accuracy fields remain `null`; detected candidates are not silently used as their own ground truth. The original annotation-based mode remains available.

## Follow-up

Keep this baseline for comparison when revising transient detection. Investigate background threshold initialization, multiple triggers within one sustained/decaying sound, and beat acquisition on noisy candidate streams. Do not simply raise the volume threshold or impose a long minimum hit gap: those changes could erase ghost notes and fills. Validate any change against independent annotations and the forthcoming drum recording, including quieter hits and cymbal-heavy playing.

No onset-detector thresholds or timing-engine scoring were changed solely to make this one non-drum recording look better.

## Subsequent ambient-noise change

After a separate live report of false starts from distant traffic, the detector was changed to require contrast against about 32 ms of preceding energy and a higher minimum level. The unchanged recording was rerun at 110 BPM using the new detector, with results saved locally in `noise-rejection-report.json` beside the original reports:

| Observation | Original | Revised |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Candidate onsets | 628 | 200 |
| Candidates in first two seconds | 68 | 14 |
| Candidate intervals under 45 ms | 393 | 28 |
| Candidates from 38.5 to 41.1 seconds | 16 | 8 |
| Automatic pause during recording | None | None |

The revised candidate stream also locks earlier, at 1.28 seconds, with a different opening phase (approximately 0.05046 seconds). The opening candidates remain unannotated, so that earlier lock is **not** evidence of an improvement in beat acquisition or scoring. Fewer candidates can include missed intended attacks. This sample remains exploratory and does not pass detection-accuracy or pause validation. The original baseline report is retained unchanged.
